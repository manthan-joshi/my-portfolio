In fall 2025 we ran a CRISPR/Cas9 knockout and knock-in at the HPRT1 locus in CHO cells. The editing arm never produced a confirmed result. Months later I went back to the raw plate data and rebuilt the whole thing as a process development report, because the interesting question was never whether the edit worked. It was why the experiment couldn't tell us either way.

What the data actually said

The selection step ran two kill curves, both 8 point, 3-fold serial dilutions in duplicate on a CellTiter-Glo readout. The 6-TG curve returned no dose response at all: relative viability between 96.2% and 103.1% across a delivered range of 0.165 to 150 µM, with the lowest reading at the second-highest dose. The puromycin curve, run six modules later and against a normal working range of 0.01 to 10 µg/mL, trended downward to 74.3% but not monotonically, and its intermediate points sit within about one pooled standard deviation of each other.

The raw signal is where it shows. Every well on both plates, live or dead, drug or vehicle, falls inside 0.042 to 0.069, which is a 1.64-fold span across thirty-two wells. Neither plate carried a no-cell blank or a full-kill control, so the floor was never established and the signal window cannot be calculated at all. The 6-TG plate still came back at 3.0% CV, which passes most repeatability criteria you would write. So the assay was repeatable, and it was repeatably measuring something other than viability.

Root cause

A protocol deviation was recorded on the day and is the correct starting point. CellTiter-Glo calls for the medium to be aspirated and the wells rinsed twice with PBS before reagent goes in, and on the 6-TG plate that was not done. Cells killed over three to four days release ATP into the medium and the rinses exist to clear it, so without them the assay reports ATP from dead cells alongside live ones. That lifts the high-dose wells and flattens the gradient.

The deviation does not explain everything, and the report says so. The puromycin plate ran later, after the analysts had committed to fixing the step, and it still reported inside the same narrow band. Correct technique produced signal of the same order as incorrect technique, so the compression is separate from the flattening. Three candidates remain: instrument configuration, since a luminescent ATP endpoint on 2,500 cells should return signal orders of magnitude above background; reagent condition, since a shared aliquot would affect both plates equally; and plate type, if clear-walled plates were used where the protocol calls for white.

Because the curve had nothing to give, the selection concentration was taken from the middle of the range rather than derived from the data. Seven days at that concentration with no medium exchange, and the untransfected control was not only alive but the most confluent well in the panel. The control that was supposed to die first was the healthiest thing on the plate.

The genotyping failed separately, and for a reason worth stating precisely. An agarose gel showed no amplification for the untransfected sample and a faint band for the control RNP, while the NanoDrop reported 26.3 and 27.8 ng/µL for those same two. A NanoDrop reads total absorbance at 260 nm and cannot distinguish amplicon from primers, nucleotides or carryover; a gel separates by size and can. Sequencing input was calculated from the NanoDrop figure, the reactions went out under-templated, and ICE recovered a 65-base window from the control trace, which is not enough to locate a 20-base protospacer. The batch failed as a unit.

On the plasmid side the two in silico predictions, 9,809 bp and 9,997 bp, cannot both describe single cuts of one circular plasmid, so they came from two different files. NotI cut and EcoRV did not, and construct identity stays unconfirmed.

Two of those failures happened the same way. A measurement nobody had checked gave a clean-looking number, something else in the same dataset said otherwise, and the clean-looking number won. That is what the report is built around.

Nobody checked whether the assay could tell a live well from a dead one before using it to pick a selection dose. Two extra wells would have answered that.

The qualification section is written against ICH Q2 principles and covers accuracy, precision, range and system suitability for the CellTiter-Glo readout, with the acceptance criteria that would have caught this before selection ran. The screening design is a resolution IV fractional factorial across six delivery parameters, with a face-centered central composite as the optimization follow-on.

What I'd change

  • Qualify the readout before the readout makes a decision. A no-cell blank and a full-kill control on every plate, with Z-factor computed and passing before anything downstream runs
  • Derive the selection concentration from the curve. If the curve is flat, the answer is to fix the assay, not to pick a number out of the middle of the range
  • Exchange selection medium rather than leaving cells for seven days, so drug depletion isn't confounded with drug resistance
  • Screen the delivery parameters as a factorial instead of one factor at a time. Cas9 to guide ratio, lipid volume, complexation time, cell density, payload mass and incubation window all interact, and sequential single-variable runs cannot see that
  • Read the gel before using a spectrophotometer to size the sequencing aliquot. The gel gates the submission, the concentration only sizes it once the gel has passed. Both measurements were already run
  • Gate amplicon release on purity as well as identity. One amplicon went out at A260/230 of 0.68, and no criterion existed to hold it
  • Register the guide and amplicon in silico before ordering primers, and submit an unedited control amplified in the same PCR run, so one weak reference cannot take down a batch
  • Run pooled and at scale rather than one locus at a time

Why this is the project I talk about

Course modules fail all the time, and a clean result from one mostly shows you followed the protocol. Going back through the raw files ten months later to work out which step lost the information took considerably longer than running it did.

I would rather be judged on that than on a knockout that worked. It is also the project I can talk through in the most detail, because I have been through every number in it twice.
