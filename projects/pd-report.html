The run failed. That's the finding.

In fall 2025 we ran a CRISPR/Cas9 knockout and knock-in at the HPRT1 locus in CHO cells. The editing arm never produced a confirmed result. Months later I went back to the raw plate data and rebuilt the whole thing as a process development report, because the interesting question was never whether the edit worked. It was why the experiment couldn't tell us either way.

What the data actually said

The selection step ran two kill curves, both 8 point, 3-fold serial dilutions in duplicate on a CellTiter-Glo readout. Neither produced a dose response. 6-TG relative viability sat between 96.2% and 103.1% across every concentration tested. Puromycin ran the same design and only reached 74.3% viability at the top of the range, at a dose that should have cleared the plate outright.

The tell is in the raw signal. Every well on both plates, live or dead, drug or vehicle, falls inside 0.042 to 0.069 RLU. Two unrelated cytotoxic agents failing in identical fashion inside the same narrow band doesn't point at the biology. It points at the readout.

Root cause

No no-cell blank and no full-kill control were run on either plate, so the background floor was never established. Without a floor you cannot tell a dead well from a live one, and every number downstream inherits that.

Because the curve had nothing to give, the selection concentration was taken from the middle of the range rather than derived from the data. Seven days at that concentration with no medium exchange, and the no-transfection control was still alive. The control that was supposed to die first didn't die at all.

Downstream, Sanger returned unreadable traces and ICE failed at the control file, which blocks every sample in the batch. On the plasmid side NotI cut and EcoRV did not, so construct identity stayed unconfirmed. The knock-in arm was lost to microbial contamination before it generated data.

Every one of those is a separate problem, but they sit on top of a single deviation: the readout was never qualified before it was used to make a decision.

A flat curve isn't a result. It's an instrument telling you it couldn't see anything, and the whole job is noticing the difference.

The qualification section is written against ICH Q2 principles and covers accuracy, precision, range and system suitability for the CellTiter-Glo readout, with the acceptance criteria that would have caught this before selection ran. The screening design is a resolution IV fractional factorial across six delivery parameters, with a face-centered central composite as the optimization follow-on.

What I'd change

  • Qualify the readout before the readout makes a decision. A no-cell blank and a full-kill control on every plate, with Z-factor computed and passing before anything downstream runs
  • Derive the selection concentration from the curve. If the curve is flat, the answer is to fix the assay, not to pick a number out of the middle of the range
  • Exchange selection medium rather than leaving cells for seven days, so drug depletion isn't confounded with drug resistance
  • Screen the delivery parameters as a factorial instead of one factor at a time. Cas9 to guide ratio, lipid volume, complexation time, cell density, payload mass and incubation window all interact, and sequential single-variable runs cannot see that
  • Gate Sanger submission on amplicon purity rather than submitting everything and hoping. One amplicon went out at A260/230 of 0.68
  • Run pooled and at scale rather than one locus at a time

Why this is the project I talk about

Every process fails somewhere. What an employer is actually buying is not a clean result, it's the ability to work out which step lost the information and what to change so it doesn't happen twice.

A clean result proves you can follow a protocol. A properly dissected failure proves you can run a process. This report is the second thing, and it's the document I'd rather be judged on.
